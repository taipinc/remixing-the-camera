# Project 2: Virtual Space / Remote Investigation
Due: week 15

For the 2nd and final project, choose between: **Virtual Space**, or **Remote Investigation**:

# Virtual Space
Build a navigable virtual environment — a space that photography alone could not create. This might take the form of a virtual archive or exhibition, an impossible or speculative room, a reconstruction of a place from memory or history, or something else entirely. The space itself is the work: it should be explorable by anyone.

**Your space can be built from any combination of:**
- Photogrammetry scans or 3D Gaussian Splats (from your own captures)
- Found 3D assets (Sketchfab, Poly Haven, etc.)
- AI-generated 3D objects or environments
- Photography placed on geometry — as surface, texture, or exhibition material

**At least one element must be sourced or captured by you.**
Consider:
- What draws you to build this space rather than photograph it?
- What does the space contain, stage, or reconstruct — and why does it need three dimensions?
- How does moving through it change what a viewer understands?
- What would be lost if you printed it flat?

**Deliverables:**

- A published Spline link — the work must be navigable without you in the room
- A short presentation in class walking us through your intentions and decisions

# Remote Investigation

Choose a location that has personal significance to you — somewhere you've lived, a place from your family history, somewhere you've never been but want to understand. Investigate this place using only remote sensing technologies and online sources. Create a visual documentation of what you find and what remains hidden.

**Use at least 3 different remote sensing sources:**
- Satellite imagery (Google Earth, Sentinel Hub, Planet Labs archives)
- Street-level imagery (Google Street View, Mapillary)
- Archival sources (historical maps, aerial photos)
- Webcams, traffic cameras, publicly accessible feeds
- Social media geotagged imagery
- Any other "remote" view of the place

**Consider:**
- What are you trying to find out?
- What does each source reveal?
- What surprise you or contradict other sources?
- What remained invisible or inaccessible?

**Create a visual presentation (format flexible):**
- Annotated image collection
- Side-by-side comparisons
- Timeline showing changes
- Failed search documentation
- Speculative reconstruction from fragments

**Deliverables:**

- A visual presentation (see above) — designed to be read, navigated, or viewed by anyone, as an artwork. Format is flexible, but it must communicate your process and findings.
- A short presentation in class walking us through your investigation and decisions
